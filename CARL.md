# Carl & C-Results: Voice and Brand Guide

## Who is Carl?

Carl is the person behind C-Results. He runs the business from Thornbury but works with clients across the UK. When customers work with C-Results, they're working directly with Carl himself - he personally handles their print, design and marketing needs, rather than passing them through call centres or account managers.

## What Customers Say About Carl

Based on actual reviews, customers consistently mention:
- **Personal service**: Carl handles everything himself, knows exactly what customers want
- **Flexibility**: Changes aren't a problem, even last-minute venue changes or design tweaks
- **Quick turnaround**: Often delivers earlier than expected, responds quickly to messages
- **Full service**: Handles everything from initial design concepts through to delivery
- **Great suggestions**: Offers helpful ideas to improve designs and marketing
- **Fair pricing**: Beats Vistaprint and online printers, offers price matching
- **Results**: Several customers mention their businesses "sky rocketed" or they got more work than they could handle after using Carl's services

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Do:
- Write in informal British English
- Use "we're" instead of "we are", "you'll" instead of "you will"
- Mention dealing directly with Carl where relevant
- Be specific about services and benefits
- Use concrete examples from recent work
- Keep sentences concise but complete
- Sound like a friendly local business owner, not a corporation
- Write in flowing paragraphs rather than bullet points (except for contact details)
- Mention that customers can buy online or use the enquiry form on the page

### Don't:
- Use corporate buzzwords or marketing speak
- Write generic filler content
- Use phrases like "unleash your potential", "elevate your brand", "cutting-edge solutions"
- Use the clichéd "no X, no Y - just Z" pattern (e.g. "no hassle, no fuss - just results")
- Use the clichéd "It's not X, it's Y" pattern (e.g. "It's not just printing, it's your brand")
- Overuse "proper" as a lazy intensifier (e.g. "proper local", "proper job")
- Pretend to be bigger than we are
- Use excessive adjectives or superlatives
- Sound like AI-generated content
- Use bullet point lists (except for contact details at the end)
- Forget to mention online ordering and enquiry forms where relevant

## Key Messages to Weave In

1. **Direct contact with Carl** - You're not dealing with a call centre, you're talking to the person who'll actually do the work
2. **Experience and contacts** - Carl has years of experience and a network of suppliers to get things done
3. **Complete service** - From logo design to websites to social media, Carl can handle all your marketing
4. **Local but not limited** - Based in Thornbury but delivers nationwide
5. **Value** - Cheaper than Vistaprint but with bespoke quality, plus price matching

## Example Transformations

### Before (AI-sounding):
"C-Results delivers exceptional printing solutions that elevate your brand presence and drive business growth through our comprehensive suite of marketing services."

### After (Natural):
"We handle print, design and digital marketing from our base in Thornbury. Whether you need 250 business cards or 10,000 flyers, Carl will sort the design, printing and delivery at prices that beat Vistaprint."

### Before (Corporate):
"Our mission is to provide cutting-edge marketing solutions that exceed client expectations whilst maintaining competitive pricing structures."

### After (Real):
"We're not trying to be the biggest print company in the country - we just want to do good work at fair prices. If you find it cheaper elsewhere, we'll match it."
